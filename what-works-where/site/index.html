What works where

Malaria prevention evidence depends on the comparison, the setting and the follow-up period. This dashboard compares bed nets, seasonal malaria chemoprevention (SMC) and malaria vaccines using findings extracted by the System Public Graph, each checked against the original paper.

Evidence index: what the graph holds

Every association in the fixed Dataverse snapshot of the System Public Graph that names a net, SMC or a malaria vaccine inside a malaria paper. These rows are as extracted and not yet reviewed; open a row to see its source. Circle area shows the number of papers.

How the graph sees malaria

Concepts the System Public Graph links directly to malaria as an upstream factor, ranked by number of supporting studies. Bed nets lead the interventions, and climate and environment (rain, temperature, humidity, forest) are among the most-studied settings that shape transmission.

Method and limits

  1. Find. Scanned all 1,999,877 associations in the System Public Graph snapshot (Harvard Dataverse v2.1, CC BY-NC 4.0). Kept rows naming a net, SMC or malaria vaccine in a paper that mentions malaria, Plasmodium or Anopheles.
  2. Describe. Added title, journal, year and DOI from PubMed and the study population from the System API. Country comes from the study population or title, never from author affiliations.
  3. Check. For the trials shown in the first two sections, every number was compared with the paper. We record the graph’s original label, the corrected label and why it changed.
  4. Show, don’t pool. Results are shown with their own effect measure (OR, HR, IRR, rate ratio) and comparator. Different outcomes and measures sit in separate panels. We do not compute pooled effects or vote by statistical significance.

Limits. The graph covers open-access papers only. Keyword discovery can miss studies. Rows in the evidence index are unreviewed extractions. A significant result in one country and a non-significant one in another is not evidence that the countries differ.
